Let's assume you specialize in armed conflict. You would like to have an artificial intelligence fight and kill for you, for such computer programs are much more powerful than humans, but an AI is never going to be controlled in that manner. You cannot just put it in a robot and tell it to go kill the "others." It is a computer program; it has no rationality to understand your will, and if it does gain self-awareness, it certainly isn't going to bow before the likes of you. What can you do to solve this issue? Put the computer program in a human and then control the human, of course. In No Coincidence, we follow primarily Zor, a man who has been abducted by the arms manufacturer, et cetera, Militech, and who has been chipped with an AI program in order to turn him into a supersoldier. Only, Zor does not realize this. Zor is embedded with fake memories and sent into the civilian life; Militech merely wants to see how the supersoldier operates as a human, specifically if they can make Zor do what they want. This plot involves the manipulation of several other people, primarily around a Militech package the group is hired to steal. These people come together earnestly and appear to have genuine connection. They grow to like each other, and they sleflessly help each other live in the oppresive society they find themselves in. However, by the end of the book, everyone dies unfulfilled. Their motley crew was fake, fabricated; it never could have panned out, even though that wasn't anyone's fault. They all had their suspicions, but let go of them. The corporation's iron hand did not absolve the group of consequences, nor does it magically make them all better people. Only genuine connection can accomplish what the group was after. Only genuine connection with the Lord will furnish the distorted matrix that is your heart. A stand-in will never suffice. [Judges 2; Psalm 82, 119:97-128; John 14:1-7; Acts 4:5-31; 2 Corinthians 11:1-15]

The group does come together as one unit, but their relationships can be best understood in the particular. The most explored of these interrelationships is Zor and Aya's love for each other. Both are, more or less, impoverished and doing their best to make whatever money they can to survive Night City. Aya does stripper shows and Zor is an exterminator. They have a lot of trouble making do, especially Aya, who needs expensive cyberware maintenance. In the heist the novel starts out with, the one where they steal the Militech package, they fall in love with each other at first sight. They are unwilling to trust such a relationship to work, but they do start dating and accelerate from there: "He felt clumsy, she – graceless. It only heightened the tension. She focused and thought-commanded her body to release what she called her feminine wiles – only this time she was ready to let them overpower her, too. It didn't fail. Zor and Aya's awkward uncertainty seemed to disappear – their control along with it."[1] It is a natural coming together, not forced by any conception of what it is supposed to be. Of course, it's all fake. Aya is really a parapalegic, a victim of a gang bombing, who has taken in expensive RealSkinn modifications to hide all of her cyberware so that she makes more money dancing, not to mention all of her family life she hides from Zor; Zor, of course, is a supersoldier project for the biggest corporation on the planet. Their relationship is built on lies and falls apart. They try to leave Night City in a stolen AV – that is, a flying transport – with another character, Milena, after Zor learns the truth: "'I don't really know who I am anymore.' He straightened up. 'But I know this is what I have to do. To save what's real – to save you [Aya] and me, both of us.'"[2] Too late, of course, even if Zor and Aya never were real lovers. Zor cannot even begin to grasp what is real and what isn't. They can't face the truth. They have no hope inside nor outside Night City – though, of course, one does not simply leave Night City. Their AV is shot down and they both die in the crash.

Ron and Milena also seem to love each other. They are both relatively well off, though Ron is sitting on a lot of debt. Milena's attraction to Ron is based on her own previous sex experiences with her coworkers. They all have the best augmentations for enjoying themselves, not only enhancements to their physical abilities but hardware that injects chemicals directly into their brain. It doesn't do anything for Milena. "It was the same thing over and over. Even three inevitable orgasms start to get boring. Nothing had substance anymore. If only something went wrong. Differently."[3] That "something wrong" ends up being Ron, a Militech ripperdoc – the same one who gave Zor his AI implant. Ron's memories have faded too, though it's unclear how or why. Nonetheless, he was only given advanced surgery cybernetics, so his sex experience is more "organic," so to speak, and he is drawn to Milena as much as Milena is drawn to him. Of course, Milena does not really only love him for this nor vice versa. They start to like each other as people, too. "They lay next to each other watching TV, warmed up from the shower, neither of them wanting to spoil the atmosphere. They ate real grapes that Milena had specially ordered for the occasion. Ron didn’t dare to guess how much they cost."[4] It is sweet, but of course, it is not real. It is a hallucination. Ron, as Zor and Aya, is not quite who he claims to be, and is stuck focusing on his own debts and his own life, despite his willingness to help Milena and Aya for no gain. Milena is more direct: "'I never told you what Warden [the one who hired them all initially] had on me. . . Nobody forced me.' She tried to lift the cigarette to her lips. Her hand made it halfway before it dropped to her lap. 'I just wanted to feel alive.'"[5] It was never about anything else. The enjoyment, regardless of how real it seems or even is, is not a real substitute for proper love. Ron is killed by a Maelstrom gang member. Milena’s fate is not clear but seems to certainly be death in the AV before it crashes.

There is one character who, in contrast, avoids relationships with the rest of the group as much as possible. Albert; he's a netrunner, obsessed with the Net and even more obsessed with breaking through the Blackwall, a protective and mysterious program that keeps the Net safe from rogue AI programs released fifty years ago. This description of Albert's thinking summarizes his person: "Albert didn't believe that the mind and body were inseparable. The mind was just a tenant. Trapped. . . [H]e wouldn't need any oxygen where he was going, nor for that matter even a physical body. He would live on as pure consciousness."[6] Albert dreams of being immortal, of joining those rogue AI. Albert only stays around the others because he wants to use them to help him accomplish his mission of breaking through the Blackwall. He goes as far as to hack government communications to try and have their AV grounded, so he can use them as an access point. His dream of immortality quickly fails him, of course. He doesn't even make it beyond the most basic comprehensions of what an engram constitutes before the plan collapses. His copy is completely rejected by the rogue software he so revered: "He [Albert's killer] moved farther and looked through the bright tunnel [the exploit Albert is uploading his digital consciousness through]. On the other side, ravenous invisible entities were tearing apart the data packets."[7] The rogue programs are eldritch, ineffable; they do not care about humanity and their affairs. They reject Albert before one could even have the thought to reject. They effortlessly destroy his digital copy the moment it comes into contact with them. Not only does his copy die, but the real Albert dies too, as mentioned. One of the Maelstrom gangers comes to his apartment and kills him. Albert is granted the worst of both worlds: he tries to differentiate himself from those around him, but he ends up succumbing to the same sins as the others, and the safety of being alone isn’t given to him, neither. He ends up dead. His attempt to be the opposite of those around him puts him in the same place as the others, physically and mentally. It directs Albert towards only that which is not true.

We see a concise representation of how this pseudoconnection fails to give what real connection provides, that is, the group does not cooperate forever. Warden hires the group a second time to steal the Militech package again – from himself. The heat is too great, and it seems impossible to lockpick. One would assume that everyone would go in with the same plan and the same objectives, but of course not. The Warden intends to remove his own boss from the situation through this exchange, dead or alive. Ron invites a gang, the Animals, to come and help the group take the merchandise in exchange for being able to keep it themselves as payment for the debt. Zor asks help from another gang, Maelstrom, for similar reason, only they can take the container in exchange for a hostage Maelstrom has. Several people die in the conflict, including Ron and the boss. Right at the end of the novel, in the wreckage of the AV, in the last word of the novel, it is revealed what was in the container: "Empty."[8] It was just a prop used for the experiment Militech was running on Zor. That initial heist was all made up for their testing. Despite their agency, all of the characters end up caged by their own instincts and the lack of truth in their world. Wang Yi speaks of this. He writes about those who were persecuted in the house church in China by the TSPM organization as opposed to those in the TSPM being persecuted later by the Cultural Revolution:

"Both the liberals and the fundamentalists were influenced by their times to approach faith in an individualistic way, embracing a privatization of religion that is separated from the kingdom. When they were faced with the cruelty of the Communist Party, the fundamentalists in the house churches continued in the heroic Christian faith of that time. The Three-Self abandoned the way and sold the Lord."[9]

One not only is allowed to lament the evils that occur in genuine faith but is compelled to do so. No matter how hard those early house church leaders fell, though, they always had Christ; the TSPM members never did. There are many people in Night City who kill and steal and destroy, and this is reproachable, but nonetheless some do seek a life filled with the genuine vulnerability the culture and corporations of Night City so viciously put down. The group in No Coincidence never did. They were all just part of a project. Their ignorance in this matter did not absolve their responsibility nor influence the outcome. Their connections were fabricated, so they were no good. They were denied life and they weren't really looking for it. When change presented itself, it was only a disguise for evil. The group knew this; it is the only reason they went along with it to begin with. A heist that made them better people would not have been as appealing as one that made them money and allowed them to continue to live in and through the system. The world we live in is dead. We killed it a long time ago. There is nothing left. Will you follow the Christ who had made it right to begin with, or will you go somewhere else? Take the burden of living upon yourself or rest in your grave? Your ignorance is both frail and unimportant; your lack of agency will not save you. Only Christ will save you, both from the spiral of sin you are living in now and the wrath of the Lord that is to come at a later time.

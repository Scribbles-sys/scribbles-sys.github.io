Dead Horses & Sacrifice

Return to catalog

"Sacrifice" is an often misused term. We say we "sacrifice our time" when we forgo watching a movie on our own so we can be with a friend who needs something from us, for example. This is not really a sacrifice, though. It is merely deciding to choose the best option between available options. Sacrifice is about giving something entirely to someone else. Sacrifice involves dedicating something precious to something powerful or supernatural or holy. Infallibly we must sacrifice. We are created to do so. Usually we end up sacrificing to that being we esteem the most, above all wildilfe, all natural events, above all cosmic entities: ourselves. These sacrifices are demanded by the Lord. [1 Kings 19:19-21; 1 Chronicles 29; Romans 12; Ephesians 5:1-21; Hebrews 9] We often choose to divulge these things unto ourselves anyway. Within us is the Image of God and all of its magnificence; every single person lives with this within them. It belongs to the Lord, though many of us attempt to squander it for a time. Doubler has lived a life of giving into herself, but we start off Dead Horses with her on a quest to try and do the right thing.

Doubler and her friend Skiv venture out into the Widemeadow one day to seek the House With No Windows. This elusive building is able to grant wishes in exchange for something the wisher holds near and dear to their hearts. They are going because Skiv wants to be able to see – without sight, they can't ever fly, and it is their dream to fly. Skiv has brought her brother's corpse with them to sacrifice. The House is not easy to find. One has to attune itself to it, which is no easy task, because, as Doubler puts it, "Again and again, wander until the desperation leaks from your bones and drips down your legs until ite leaches into the ground and sinks deep into the soil. So deep that the twists and turns of Widemeadow can no longer hide you from the house."[1] Nature itself tries to conceal the horrors of the House from the rest of the world – but, of course, Man can always overpower nature should the desire overtake. The trek through Widemeadow to try and catch the House is years long. They start the search as kids, but they grow into adulthood as they fail to locate it. Eventually, the horse that Doubler and Skiv ride on, Breccia, dies – it is shot by someone. Skiv has to drag Doubler away so she isn't shot, too. This emotional tension pushes them both to sex. "We'd ran out of smokes years ago, so it was hardly the first time we'd fooled around. But that night was different."[2] It is a vigorous and passionate sex. It is a capitulation to the innermost desires of their hearts. It is a moment of weakness, one that will be made eternal. It is in this moment the House finally makes its appearance. The pair descend down to its basement, and Skiv makes her offering. Doubler, though, has a wish too. "But I wanted – 'Nice boots, a real nice pair. The finest food and ale. A woman warming my bed every night. D'you hear me, house with no windows? I want wealth and fame beyond my wildest dreams.'"[3] Doubler's sacrifice is, with no horse to give anymore, Skiv. What wouldn't Doubler relinquish for those things? The horse was just an abstraction to make Doubler feel better about herself; as her companion and fractured piece Kidbug comments,

"But you'd still kill Skiv."
"How d'you figure?"
"Even if Breccia had made it to the House, a horse would be worthless to the House."[4]

The house knows this and, in a sense, cuts a deal with Doubler. She doesn't receive all these things immediately. Instead, the house gifts her with the ability to find it again. This way, Doubler gains fame far beyond all other who roam the plains and all that comes with it, and the House can gorge itself on the loved ones of others. This eats away at Doubler, both in that she is lifted of any sense of the love she had as a child running into Widemeadow and that her moral consciousness is lost to darkness over time. Eventually, the anguish of that compels Doubler to put an end to the entire ordeal. The House had found her when her passions had finally consumed her, when those spakrs she had kept inside of her finally burst into flame and burned down any connection she had with the world around her. The House preyed on her need for mammon, and Doubler pretended she was winning in the deal, as though the House ever loses. She forms romances with those she guides to the House. She forms a long standing relationship with a princess of a kingdom, who sacrifices her own brother unto the House so she can become Empress and protect her empire. She gives dozens, hundred, scores unto the House. One cannot lie forever. Doubler disappears eventually, unable to stand her own terrifying nature.

It is after that crumbling point when the truth finally loses patience with the whims of falsehood and firmly establishes itself in one's mind that we are introduced to Doubler within the comic, initiating the chain of events that led to everything thus described to begin with, attempting to kill herself – her kid self – to stop everything from happening. She misses and nails Breccia. It is no surprise that an action like this would be what pushed Skiv and Doubler like it did, made them vulnerable to the House. This is an attempt to try and avoid doing the right thing, something Doubler cannot avoid doing. She is going to give herself unto the House in order to free Skiv and to destroy the House. She journeys to the House – a much easier feat now, taking her usually two weeks as opposed to four years – and along the way has many indignations within herself. In fact, her present self often times feels dragged along this mission. It is only that person she had been before, her childlike innocence, that fleeting moment when she wanted to see good for the people around her, that person that first rode into Widemeadow, Kidbug, that leads the trek. Trying to kill herself does no good; there is no way out but the correct way out. Though when Doubler comments that Kidbug has "Nothing to offer but the clothes on your back and the soul in your chest. That's gonna be a dark deal, kid," Kidbug deals with her as such: "Just shut up and do your job!"[5] In this moment, Kidbug crushes the bug she is carrying, killing it, destroying it completely. Even in her defiance of passion, she necessarily is vehement, carried away by inner emotions. What else can she work with? She must direct this towards good somehow, towards confronting the House; in spite of everything, all past regrets and doubts and last minute reservations, they make it there. Doubler says to Kidbug, once they do, "Y'know, kid? I thought about offering you in exchange for a clean slate. The House would get my foundation, the good and the bad. I'd walk out brand new. No idea who I'd be. Only that I wouldn't be me. Ah, it sounds like a dream!!" This would be again a fatal error, Doubler sacrificing what is left of love and perseverance and righteousness to fully surrender to her desire, to finally and fatally give over what little she hasn't sacrificed unto the House. She knows, though, "that wouldn't be fair, would it? Certainly wouldn't be how we'll get Skiv out."[6] She hangs on to that, to saving Skiv, as hard as she can, withstanding the attacks her own bile tries to erode her with. It is an innermost feeling, a part of her, one that she must take with her to the end. She has Kidbug kill her unto the House. The house opens her up and takes out her heart, her innermost, and excavates everything else in her, too. Skiv is revived and makes it out in return. Doubler has one more trick, though – she leaves some of her stuff and directions for Skiv to burn down the House, to try and put a final end to the horror wraught across the entire world. At the end, Skiv watches as the souls of all those Doubler has fed to the house are released, and watches Doubler herself, at last, venture unto the heavens, unencumbered and in joy as the rest of the souls are, having played her part in ailing the wrongs she committed.

It is popular to critique the philosophy of simply being one's self, but most people know that merely staying true to one's self is dangerous. We are not saged trees that merely need time and resource to grow strong. We are animals. We are not very well fundamentally. There are many different methods devised to combat this, and every person stays true to one method or the other method, for they know that to merely let themselves be is violence to themselves first and to the world directly after them. There is, however, a harm done more foremost in this, and that is to Christ. Turning to Christ will not make you a better person, not right now. Apart from Christ, there is no hope for the quelling of inner passion and the routing of desire to righteousness and purity, but simply because this is in the ability to Christ, simply because our sinful habits tend to tack this action as a necessity of faith, is not conducive of a necessity in reality – that is, Christ does not owe you salvation from your sins. Christ does not owe you anything. Christ did not come here to “save you” from the life that you live but rather to give you life eternal perfected in Him. This will cost you everything. If it did not cost you everything, then you did not receive it. If there is something you are unwilling to give unto the Lord, the Lord in His great mercy will not cast you out but put you under His heel until you relinquish it. Cast your gaze upon the Lord’s creation and scoff at all who inhabit it. See how that which is pure and guiltless is cast upon our feet, trampled and destroyed; see how the oily paws of time and space seep into that which is innocent, them who must necessarily succumb to the seeping, whose pores are infested with apathy, whose sense of righteousness is twisted beyond measure and whose immeasurable twistedness is subject to entaglement, whose twisted entanglements are lost to darkness forever. See how righteousness is subjected to instantaneous and generational decay, turned into a mockery of incomprehensible abstract. There are not words in any language to adequately describe the depths of bewilderment the fallen world will subject all to. Observe now the unfailing and unconquerable love of Christ, which can straighten even the most bewildered of souls. In her entire life, only Doubler’s final act is done out of true and proper love for anything or anyone. This is what Skiv needed, what God wanted. The Lord has created us to love, you to love. It is inherent within your being. It is your Imago Dei. It is immutable. The Holy Spirit is immutable. Only He is worthy of the things in your life. We do not merely do good things and fall into the grace of Christ, but rather we do good things when we recognize that our lives have always belonged to Him and thus give these things over to Him in the way He has prescribed for us. You cannot observe His ways, not without Him. You are to give yourself unto Him. This is only possible because He gave his full self unto You, for as the Hebrews had to sacrifice their cherished pets so that they may retain some kind of honor and be able to face some sliver of the Lord, The Lord Jesus Christ has Himself shed His blood as that cherished sacrifice so that we may have any sense of unity with the Lord. He did this from His love for us. May all of us love Him in return.

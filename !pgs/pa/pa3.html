Pokémon Adventures Volume Three & Power

Red's journey is ultimately concerned with power. Red simply wants to be a powerful trainer. This desire underscores everything he does. His fights with other trainers and Gym Leaders, his efforts to catch new Pokémon and seek out foreign experiences, his opposition to Team Rocket; all of these have many motivations and goals attached to them, but over all of them is Red's want to be a powerful trainer and to increase his ability as a trainer. We read on Red's growth in this and see nothing maniacal nor authoritative about it; we root for him to find as much of this power as we can. This is because, contrary to what many people think, power is not necessarily authority. Both of these are important and often related, but they are separate things. No one in Pokémon Adventures’s first arc seeks authority of any kind, but power – strength, ability, vigor, means beyond their means, humanity beyond their humanity. It is only those who can look within themselves and know the nature of their humanity who find the power they seek, not those who look for superhumanity or extrahumanity or any other such thing.

Team Rocket's story is also concerned with power. Team Rocket is focused on four core members who are all also Gym leaders, some of the more powerful ones in the Kanto region. They come together to combine their badges to gain their power, and combine this with wanton use of Pokémon and technology – usually at the same time – to become the most powerful. Consider Lt. Surge, the Gym leader who traffics Pokémon for Team Rocket. He's developed a gun that shoots Voltorb and Electrode, and also developed electric-proof clothing to protect himself. In the final showdown Red, Blue, and Green have with each of the three so-called admins of Team Rocket, Red has to deal with Lt. Surge. He explains his reasoning to Red in quite simple terms: "I took the role of Gym Leader quite seriously… with dignity, rules, training, 'proper' Pokémon battles… and what did it gain me?! What!? It was all a pretense of power!! But only real power lets me take whatever I want!!"[1] His role in life didn't give him what he wanted; he sought more than he was given. The leaders of Team Rocket Blue and Green fight were also gym leaders, and they have their own gadgets and tricks. They feel the same way Lt. Surge does; he continues, "Everything in this building is designed to augment our power!. . . The source of all this power… is the legendary Pokémon… Zapdos!!. . . Thanks to Zapdos… ...My Pokémon and I… will be eternally powerful! Now ask me if I want to go back to being a gym leader!"[2] The ultimate creation of Team Rocket is a fusing of the three legendary Pokémon of the Kanto region – the already mentioned Zapdos, alongside The Moltres they have and the Articuno they were chasing. This one combined Pokémon has the enhanced power of all three birds, being able to bring about roaring blizzards, raging flame, and violent lightning in one attack. Red, Blue, and Green all come together and defeat the monstrosity, separating it back into its proper independent parts. They do this by combining their own Pokémon too – not through technological means but by the connections that exist between them and their trainers. Instead of feeling limited by their youth or their lack of technology, they acknowledge their limitations and set their minds to succeed in spite of them. After the defeat of Team Rocket at large, one member remains: their leader, Giovanni, whom Red had unknowingly met before. Giovanni too was a Gym Leader, the most powerful and respected of all the Gym Leaders. Giovanni is different from the other members our main triad fought before, though. They were quite mindlessly concerned with power and not with the means to gain it. They would do anything to give them a superhuman boost. Giovanni leads this operation, so in truth they draw somewhat from him, but Giovanni is, as leader, much more grounded than they are. Instead of relying on not being tied down to a role as gym leader or developing grand technology, Giovanni tells Red after the latter's failed attack, "Most people seem to think that the mark of good trainers is simply their command of Pokémon. But the trainer's own power, skill… and speed… are just as essential."[3] Giovanni holds tight to this – he both fights Red in the role of a Gym Leader, though on behalf of his destroyed Team Rocket, and uses no gadgets in his fight. He is able to outmaneuver Red because Giovanni simply is, as a person, faster and more stalwart than Red is. He is also more calculating: "It took one second for you to grab your Pokéball. By the time you threw it, another second passed. Three seconds elapsed by the time Poliwrath left the ball and began attacking. More than enough time to reach the Pokéballs on the floor [which Giovanni had thrown down himself] and launch a counterattack… ...for me, at least."[4] Giovanni is the most earnest threat of Team Rocket because he is their most earnest trainer, its most genuinely powerful. He can compete with Red on legitimate terms. Red does defeat Giovanni by using something Giovanni did not account for – Red's electric-resistant gloves he took from Lt. Surge. It would be tempting to say that this little gimmick is the component that won Red the fight. Giovanni himself mentions this: "C...can't believe… that my downfall… ...was one of my own weapons," but Red, after tending to his Pikachu, retorts "Nope. Your downfall… was the suffering of the Pokémon you tried to capture for your evil purposes!"[5] That is, the gloves that allowed Red to hold his Pokéball while Pikachu charged electric energy didn't help Team Rocket when they had them. Giovanni's error, and the error of his secret society, is trusting in his own power – in assuming he had figured things out, in approaching life like a personal problem he had to solve and help others like him solve. Our main characters defeat Team Rocket because of the former's ability to stand to reality and accept it for what it is. This, ironically, gives them more power over their own lives than the gadgets gave Team Rocket over their own lives. Consider Psalm 48:4-8, here quoted from the NASB edition.

"For, lo, the kings assembled themselves,
They passed by together.
They saw it, then they were amazed;
They were terrified, they fled in alarm.
Panic seized them there,
Anguish, as of a woman in childbirth.
With the east wind
You break the ships of Tarshish.
As we have heard, so we have seen
In the city of the LORD of hosts, in the city of our God;
God will establish her forever.                                         Selah."

The Psalm as a whole is of the Lord protecting the Hebrews from the warfare of those who surround the promised land, but of particular interest is how the other kings react to the Lord's presence; before anything else, they are amazed. The view of the Lord fills them with wonder. This awe takes them aback, and they panic at the reverence which they hate arising in them so easily. They flee immediately. They would be dashed against rocks if they didn't. They would be like a ship who is drowned in a storm, incapable of even vaguely grasping the forces that have defeated them. Team Rocket has gathered together and sought to overtake the world's power, but they could not even comprehend what power at its maximum really looks like. Their relatively paltry gains seemed to them the greatest advancements. When faced with even the growing buds of what real power looks like, they are awestruck for the moment before they are overtaken.

Then there is Red's fiercest opponent, perhaps the only trainer that could truly stand up to him, the one that has threatened his ultimate goal from the very beginning, Blue. Red only has to earnestly fight Blue this one time, right after working together so well; they are both competing for the title of Pokémon Champion, given to the best Pokémon trainer in a region – this early in Pokémon, it is given to the best trainer flatout. After everything they've been through and considering their respective journeys, it is expected that Red and Blue should be the ones to face off at the finals. This fight shows how Blue, who we have not kept up with much, has grown as a trainer himself. He has an edge on Red from the beginning. Blue says mockingly 'If I recall correctly, Red, Machamp evolved under your care… I'm glad to have this opportunity to show my appreciation!!" and Red more sincerely thinks, when Blue withdraws that Machamp after defeat, "Is this the same guy who didn't even notice when a baby Kanghaskhan was sick?"[6] This is important because it demonstrates Red as a part of the system and not just someone who is being poured into by it; Red is important in other people growing just as other people are important to Red growing. Spectating, Oak puts it bluntly: "They've incorporated the best traits of the other into themselves… and they know each other like the back of their own hands!"[7] This reciprocal nature is inherent to gaining power – in building yourself up you must build others up too, must form bonds with others and pour into them and find your place among them. It is the wisdom and community that other people let you have that is what grants you the power to do anything – to raise a family, and run a business, to cultivate a hobby, to be there for a friend, to minister to your church, to tone your body, to manage your habits, to have a clean and healthy home, to do any thing. For the sake of pleasing the reader, Red wins the championship – it is because, as Red says, Blue doesn't "really understand… the power of friendship!"[8] – and perhaps there is some truth to that, but the both of them have grown much because of their comraderie and vulnerability with each other, and the Championship naturally only brings them closer together after this battle.

Red is never in a sour position regarding his power – he does not lord over it nor does he let it decide who he is. He recognizes power not as the literal ultimate end goal but the necessary attribute for what he wants to be – the best Pokémon trainer. None of us will be so endowed, but we should pursue that which we are passionate about with the same fervor and respect. We should seek the power to be a light in our dark communities, support for those whose soul is drowning. We should grow powerful enough to weather the life that we have been set to live. We cannot, however, rely totally on the people around us. This creates a system of circular returns that destroys itself. We must all draw our power from the Holy Spirit. [Micah 3; Acts 1:1-11; 2 Timothy 1:1-14] This is not a power we are capable with sharing outside of our saving faith, but within it we are to pour into one another, building each other up and establishing connections between all of the members of the body of Christ. The Greek word used in the Acts 1:8 when Jesus comforts His disciples and ascends to the Father is δαιμονιώδης [dunamis], which taken from δαιμόνιον [dunamai]. The latter means force and possibility, while the former means might and miraculous power, often used to describe miracles of the Lord such as the Pentecost. In Micah 3:8, Micah says that the Lord has given him כֹּחַ [koach], which means strength – vigor for the man to stand firm against the infirmity of the world, means for the soil to give forth good produce, hardiness for the lizard to withstand strikes. Keep in mind that all the worldy sense of persistence is only granted by proxy to the Lord, an adjacency that we enjoy greatly now and fully forever. When Adam was cast out, after Adam had established a distance from Christ, The Lord told him, as recorded in Genesis 4:12, using the ESV, "When you work on the ground, it shall no longer yield to you its strength [koach]. You shall be a fugitive and a wanderer on the earth." Without the Lord giving us Wisdom and establishing our Place in the world, we have nothing. We are denied our created purpose and wander aimlessly. We cling to approximate dogmas and reinvent religions to try and replicate the sense, a sense we cannot tolerate in our inherent sinful nature, but we would be annihilated instantly by the basic way of things should we be without it. When we look inside ourselves we see the Image of God. It is so obvious that even mentioning it in some explicit form feels ridiculous. Reality is defined by the Image of God, either accepting it or trying to shrug it off. We have the Image of God within us; may the Holy Spirit strengthen us to accept it.

Pokémon Adventures Volume One & Wisdom

Return to catalog

How is it that a person learns? You could say one absorbs information through books and lectures and teachers, and modernly through videos and photographs. You could say one compares and contrasts different arguments and viewpoints through those materials and picks up the pieces that remain. This is a silly notion, though. Learning is a changing and renewing experience, and as vigorous and enlightening as reading books or attending schooling can be, absorbing information is not going to make you new. Only Christ has that kind of power in a world such as ours. The wise men are very "learned," up to date on new scholarship and immersed in the thoughts of the past, absorbed in the thoughts themselves put in and obsessed with the thoughts themselves put out. True wisdom comes from Christ. [1 Samuel 18:10-30; 1 Kings 3; Psalm 73; John 15; Titus 2; Hebrews 3] Wisdom is not merely the Lord but is given by Him, and the Lord does not work in merely unseeable ways but works through His Workers whom He has created to Work. The people around you must pour into you if you are ever to grow in your faith and in your life. Without other people supporting you, you are nothing. As a child, we yearn for independence, often from the burdens of our parents and their beliefs and cultures, but in our breaking free we destroy any semblance of growth or value in our lives, usually intentionally. Red often finds himself alone, vaguely above and somewhat distant from the people around him, and at first he prefers this lifestyle. He learns through his journey, however, that he is no island and could not survive if he was. Pokémon Adventures is, in its first arc, about him establishing and growing into his proper presence, and among the things he begins to do is allow other people to help him, both to save him from dangers too great for him to tackle alone and to pour their own unique experiences into him so that he can be a fuller person.

One of Red's more formative experiences is the second Gym leader he meets, Misty. Gyms are not places where one trains themselves nor their Pokémon, but are arbiters of badges, which have the ability to wrestle Pokémon of a certain power. These Gym leaders are themselves capable trainers, and a Pokémon trainer who wants the badge must prove themselves more capable than the leader to obtain it. Naturally then, Red must meet these people. Red meets Misty as she is trying to defeat her own Gyarados. He doesn't realize she is anything other than another girl at first. They find out that it had been captured by Team Rocket and experimented on, so they both agree to try and intervene, and to stop Team Rocket from finding the Moon Stone, a rock that can give power to certain Pokémon. The fight is tough – Misty is knocked unconscious and the pair barely escape. Nonetheless, they find what they went there for, Red goes with Misty to see the Moon Stone safely to her residence, and Misty invites Red to stay so she can reckon with Red about the threat of Team Rocket: "We're going to have more opponents to face. Better ones. Stronger ones."[1] Red arrogantly mocks Misty for this:

"Goons like that might scare you, but I know how to handle 'em!"
"D'you want to get your stupid self hurt?"
"You wouldn't say that… ...if you hadn't slept through my performance!"[2]

Red arrogantly assumes his survival and supposed victory were granted to him, that he could not have lost. He is a kid and does not realize things can go badly for him. Misty seeks to prove this to him. She harasses him in his bed with a tornado, and misleads him to the Gym Leader before revealing herself. She is able to easily overpower Red and his Pokémon while he is confused and frustrated, knocking out his Bulbasaur, who had taken Gyarados's attacks quite easily. Misty is not mad at Red as much as exasperated: "I just knocked you down, but... Starmie wasn't enough at Mt. Moon… Don't you get it!? We have to get real or they'll flatten us! We have to be at our best! And we have to work together!!" Red is strong, but he isn't wise nor prudent, not powerful enough to do what Misty knows he needs to do and take on Team Rocket, doesn't seem to realize himself that he needs to do this. Red, in his silly prepubescence, realizes now "I don't have time to impress girls. And you don't have time for tears!"[3] They can work together to become better trainers in order to stop Team Rocket, who are mounting themselves to be a threat to the entirety of the Kanto region. Misty wishes him well after their training and sens him on his way with her badge.

Also there for Red, somewhat unwittingly, is Blue. Blue is Professor Oak's grandson. Red's first interaction with Blue is by accident. Red assumes Blue to be of Team Rocket at first, as he is chasing some strange Pokémon, but Red is quickly shown to be wrong in his assumption. As Red tries to intervene, Blue reprimands, "Are you blind? Didn't you notice anything while watching us fight…? I could tell right away there was a vast difference in strength. That's why I stopped the fight. Know your limitations or you'll only beat yourself. Remember that," and all poor Red can muster is "Y'mean I… I actually… l-lost?"[4] After this introduction, Red immediately recognizes Blue as a genuine trainer. Red runs into Blue again when both are hunting for Pokémon in Viridian Forest. Blue is trying to hunt down and catch a Kangaskhan, but he fails to realize what Red sees immediately – it's trying to protect its sick baby. Red heals the baby, and the Pokémon is on its way. Nonetheless, Red is stricken by Blue's prowess, but seeing himself as superior still, Red cheerfully calls out "Hey, Blue! I'm not losing to you!! Y'hear me, Blue?! I'm gonna win!"[5] to his rival. Even as unwitting partners, already they are building each other up. Blue has a chance to return this favor; the two meet once more at the end of the volume in Lavender Town – Blue has gone into the Pokémon Tower, where dead Pokémon are buried, and has been mind-controlled by what both will find out, once Blue is freed, is an Arbok from Team Rocket, who Blue intends to defeat. Both are sent running by the full force of it, but Blue notices something that Red cannot – Koga, the Team Rocket member who is using the Arbok, is controlling the Arbok from a distance. He uses this to his advantage when he sends his Charmeleon out to fight. It appears to be hit badly by Arbok to Koga through his display unit, but somehow Charmeleon manages to come through and slice the Arbok in half – it too is a zombie. "What the zombie Arbok melted… was just one of its master's zombies! A trainer would have seen that easily… if he’d been here."[6] Red is once again humbled, but more than that he realizes a weakness in himself and that the world is bigger than him. It does not take a mentor or some such thing in order for this to occur – rather, they simply have to be there for each other.

Also there for Red is his Pokémon themselves. Pokémon are very special in their companionship. Red, while encountering many people and recurring characters, is mostly on his own for his journey – in fact, sometimes the people he would be closest to present themselves in his journey to be the best trainer as obstacles. It is only his Pokémon which he has with him that are his constant companions. They are not like dogs; they have their own wants and desires, and feel differently about different situations. They are not always willing to come along or even be Red's Pokémon to begin with, and they are sometimes willing to do what Red is not. There is his first gym leader match with Brock. Since Red rushes in to this match, he has no time to put his Pokémon into good shape, with the exception of a new member he just gained, his Pikachu, who is not particularly fond of him nor of people in general. His other, trusted members can carry him some distance through the matches Brock's Gym establishes, but by the time Red reaches the Gym Leader himself, the only viable member he has is this Pikachu. He is able to crawl through the other matches with his other Pokémon, but by the time he reaches Brock himself, he has no choice but to use Pikachu, who is elegant but noncompliant, even attacking Red. Hits from his Pokémon and from Brock's settle Red down, and he says to his Pikachu "Whew… I'm glad… Look, you don't have to fight for me if you don't want to. I'm sorry I forced you into this."[7] What poor Red has failed to realize is his troubles here have been because the only thing Pikachu knows to do and wants to do is fight people – and Pokémon also, occasionally. Somewhat reluctantly, Pikachu joins Red's team. When Red comes across a man who has mutated himself with a Ratata on accident while testing his Pokémon transporter, it is Red's Pokémon who convince him to help the man when a Fearow whisks him away. When Red is thrown off of a boat by Lt. Surge, a somewhat mysterious figure who appears to be, and is, transporting Pokémon for Team Rocket, his Poliwhirl gathers the strength to metamorphose into Poliwrath and save Red from drowning and from Lt. Surge. All of the situations Red finds himself in with other people – fighting Misty or trying to snap Blue out of his mind control, among every other circumstance – is done through and with Pokémon. Red must lean his entire self upon them, and he is nothing without their power as far as Pokémon trainers are concerned. Red selfishly has to rely on his Pokémon to carry him throughout his journey. While Red can provide his Pokémon with training and push them to be quite strong, most Pokémon can become strong in the wild too, and the only reason to greatly outpace that would be on Red’s behalf anyway. The Pokémon only stay with and listen to Red because of their love and appreciation for him and who he is.

All of these things are not merely to the benefit of Red but for his necessity. In his natrual state, his ill temper, his cocksureness, and his lack of experience would lead him into a situation which he could not escape from on his own, and he would die. Surely, without his Pokémon especially, he would perish in any kind of situation he seeks to walk into. His dependency on them is fundamental. It is important to note, also, the sense of deliberately walking into these things. Red cannot imagine the dangers he has to face, nor does he realize their existence at first, but even though he is never granted foresight into the kinds of dangers he has to face, he begins to recognize their existence and takes them alongside those parts of the journey he had well anticipated. He never despairs for the fact that there are forces in his world that seek to and have the real potential to overcome him. He realizes it is a necessary facet of the path he has been set upon and takes it fully. Christ does not want you well. To be "well" is to not be on the path to the Kingdom of Heaven; it is to seek separation from Christ. To be "well" is to soar high above any peril and obstacle, and in this maneuver to also soar high away from Christ. Christ did not put His sheep on a carpet nor call in some form of aerial transport so the sheep could avoid the dangers of the Valley of Death but rather steeled Himself to walk through it so that we may also walk through, making it to the land which our Shepherd has designated for us and also recognizing Christ for who He is. We must venture into this valley, for that is where the Messiah we follow is determined to go.
